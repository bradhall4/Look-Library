# Look Library — the product rule

Read alongside `config.md`. Applies to every run from look 004 onward.

## Why

The library exists to adapt art direction to commercial imagery. A look proved only on
unbranded subjects proves half the job. Every entry now marries the treatment to a real product
category, so the frames read as raw imagery from an actual shoot rather than as an art exercise.

## The rule

Each run picks **one product**, from **tech, beauty, fashion or food**, appropriate to the world
the look already lives in. Not random: it should be something that could plausibly be shot in
that setting, by that photographer, for a real client.

- Sport or street look -> a running shoe, performance eyewear, a sports drink, a phone
- Night or club look -> smart glasses, a camera, a can, a lighter
- Portrait or beauty look -> eyelashes and skin, a lipstick, a fragrance bottle, earbuds
- Domestic or table look -> a sandwich, a soft drink, a condiment, cookware
- Travel or outdoors look -> a backpack, sunglasses, a watch, a vehicle detail

Rotate the category across runs. Do not do phones three times running.

## How the product appears

- In **two of the four frames**, not all four. One frame stays clean so the look itself is
  legible without commercial interference, and the control frame is always the clean one.
- Held, worn, used or resting in the scene as it naturally would be. Not floating, not
  hero-lit on a plinth, not centred like a pack shot unless the look itself is a still life.
- The treatment string is unchanged. The product enters through the subject clause only. That
  is the whole point: proof the recipe survives a commercial object.
- No added type, no logos composed into the frame, no packaging graphics, no campaign design.
  These are raw shoot outputs, not layouts.

## Describe form, not brand

**Write the product by its physical characteristics rather than its brand name.** This is a
craft rule before it is a caution:

- Image models render real logos and wordmarks badly. A mangled almost-logo makes an otherwise
  good frame unusable and reads as cheap.
- The brand's silhouette is usually what carries recognition anyway. "A slim glass and aluminium
  phone, matte sage, wide horizontal camera bar across the upper back" reads as the right object
  without a single letterform.
- It keeps the output honest. These are look tests, not mockups of a real brand's campaign.

Good: "a breaded chicken fillet in a plain white paper wrapper, waxed paper, pickle visible".
Bad: "a Chick-fil-A sandwich".

Good: "wraparound sport sunglasses, single curved lens, matte black frame, iridescent coating".
Bad: "Oakleys".

Where a brand is genuinely the point, name the category and the distinguishing form, then comp
the real product in afterwards in post. The generated frame is the lighting and world test.

## Recording it

Write the product into the `product` column of `public.looks` as the short form description,
plus the category in plain words, e.g. `smartphone — slim glass and aluminium, matte sage`.
The site shows it on the index tile and in the detail header.

## In the verdict

Say whether the product survived the treatment. This is the new failure mode worth watching:
some looks flatten a product into the scene until it is unreadable, and some treatments fight
a product's own material qualities. Both are useful to know before a look reaches a pitch.
